FAQ

Frequently asked questions

The questions every Master's applicant asks us first.

Many public universities in Germany do not charge traditional tuition fees for many Master's programmes, but this does not mean studying is completely free. Students still need to budget for semester contributions, living expenses, health insurance, accommodation and other costs. Some universities and some German states or programmes may charge tuition fees for certain international students.

Yes. Germany has many Master's programmes taught entirely or primarily in English, especially at the Master's level. Availability varies by university and subject, so check the exact programme language requirements rather than assuming every programme is available in English.

There is no single grade requirement for all German Master's programmes. Universities may consider your final grade, relevant coursework, ECTS or credit requirements, subject background, language skills and other programme-specific requirements. Some programmes are highly competitive and may effectively require stronger grades than their formal minimum.

APS is an academic document-verification process required for applicants from certain countries, notably India and China in relevant study-visa or application contexts. Whether you need APS depends on your nationality and situation. Verify the current requirements for your country before applying.

You generally need to prove that you can finance your stay in Germany. The required amount is updated by the German authorities, so always check the current requirement before your visa application at Make it in Germany.
